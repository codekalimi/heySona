Analogy: the derivative is the speedometer reading at one instant (how fast position changes right now). An integral is the odometer: adding up all the tiny distances.

Tiny example: f(x) = x^2 at x = 3 has slope 6 (show it with a tangent line).

Draw it: a plotted curve; a tangent line as a "plot" of "f(a) + slope*(x - a)" with a slider for a, so the user drags the touching point and watches the slope change. Show the slope value live in a card. For the chain rule, show gears turning at different speeds (rates multiply).

Key insight: a derivative is a slope of a curve at a point; the gradient (many inputs) points uphill, which is why gradient descent walks the opposite way.

Common confusion: dy/dx is not a fraction you can always cancel; read it as "how much y changes per tiny change in x".
