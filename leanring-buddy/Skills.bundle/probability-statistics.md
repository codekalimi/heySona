Analogies: probability is the long-run fraction of coin flips. Bayes' rule is updating a guess when evidence arrives (a positive test for a rare disease is still probably a false alarm). Variance is how spread out the guesses of a crowd are.

Draw it: bar charts from rects (bind heights to a slider, e.g. a coin's bias p), a bell curve with "plot" ("exp(-(x-m)^2/(2*s^2))" with sliders m and s), and a 10 x 10 grid of small circles colored to show "37 out of 100".

Key insights: expected value is the average outcome over many tries. Conditional probability changes the "out of what" (the denominator). More samples shrink the noise in an average (roughly by the square root).

Common confusion: P(A given B) is not P(B given A) — show both with the 100-dot grid.
